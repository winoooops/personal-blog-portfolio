When we talk about AI, most conversations revolve around _creativity_: can machines be creative, can they solve problems in original ways, can they replace human ingenuity?

As Dr. Po-Shen Loh (Carnegie Mellon University) points out, the answer is increasingly **yes**. AI has solved Olympiad-level math problems designed to be novel and resistant to rote techniques. In other words, even the pinnacle of human "creative problem-solving" is no longer uniquely ours.

So where does that leave us?

Dr. Loh makes a compelling case that the _real human edge_ is **thoughtfulness**. And after reflecting on his talk, here's how I see it connecting to my own journey.

## Problem-Solving with LLMs: Language as Human Thinking

Counterintuitively, working with LLMs (like ChatGPT) has reminded me that **reading, writing, and expressing ideas with logic** is the foundation of human thought.

LLMs are pattern recognizers; they generate "most probable answers." But real problem-solving means wrestling with a situation we've never seen before.

Dr. Loh's advice — _keep generating solutions, then try to destroy them until you find one that's invincible_ — is a reminder that we can't outsource the _struggle_.

That iterative destruction-and-rebuilding is what strengthens our mental "muscle," and it's where true learning happens.

## The Human Role: Collaboration and Empathy

If AI can do the tasks of individuals, what's left for humans? **Collaboration.**

Entrepreneurship and teamwork, at their core, are about creating value for others. And the only way to create meaningful value is through empathy — what Loh calls _"simulating the world."_

When I design a system, an algorithm, or even a business idea, the root always comes from imagining real-world problems and putting myself in someone else's shoes.

AI can help internalize the logic, but it can't _care_. Thoughtfulness is what makes the design human.

## Rethinking Education: Connection as the Curriculum

Loh's educational model is strikingly simple: let charismatic high schoolers mentor younger students in critical thinking. Why? Because **education is not just about information, it's about connection.**

The real value of education is building a _robust human network_ — people you can trust, people you can call on when challenges arrive.

In a world where information is cheap (and often biased), the ability to build resilient communities is priceless.

## The Danger: Losing Thoughtfulness and Truth

Over-reliance on AI is seductive. It saves time, produces polished outputs, and sometimes makes us forget the _fun_ of creating.

But if we outsource every act of writing, designing, or storytelling, we also outsource our individuality — the "hint of our favor" that makes ideas human.

AI also raises the stakes of deception. Narratives can be polished, persuasive, and deeply biased. Without critical thinking — without the ability to _simulate the world ourselves_ — we risk being swept away by whichever story sounds best.

## Social Entrepreneurship: Value = Problem × Care

One of Loh's sharpest points: a great entrepreneur makes a **net profit on every single person they help**.

That doesn't just mean money. It means _delivering real value to real problems that people care enough about to "pay" for — financially, emotionally, or socially._

Too often, we chase elegant solutions without asking: _what does this problem actually cost?_ If a problem has cost, it has value. If it has value, then solving it creates a sustainable path forward.

## My Takeaway

In the AI era, creativity alone isn't enough. AI can already match or surpass us there.

What will truly matter is **thoughtfulness**:

- The discipline to think critically instead of leaning on shortcuts.
- The empathy to simulate others' perspectives and care about outcomes.
- The courage to create value in collaboration with others.

If we hold on to those traits, AI doesn't replace us — it amplifies us.
